# Retrospective

## What worked

- For food and other addictions such as smoking, the best approach that has worked for me is not having it in the house. Works the best and I stop thinking about it all the time. If it is in the house, I keep having thoughts about it and it is very easy to give in (nearly always based on how addictive it is).
- I started being aware and reduced my phone usage by `Digital Wellbeing` and putting timers. This has for sure reduced my phone time. Youtube still sometimes consumes a lot of my time, i removed timers since some courses and "productive" content happens there but a lot of times it gets taken over watching unproductive content.
- Methylphenidate works great and it is by far the best improvement. Taking it daily certainly loses a decent bit of the benefits of the medicine (building tolerance).
- Outlook reminders and meetings/calendars have made it very easy for me in Work (even though they sometimes stop due to bugs which is very problematic). I tried Google calendars and some other approaches but they dont effectively remind me of things (untrustworthy and buggy)
- Exercising free will has reduced the "depression" or "existential dread" part a lot. Choosing what I do has improved everything and my productivity inside and outside work. I also automatically reduced my Gaming time (also happily) when I get to work on things I choose.
- Essentially the equation of burnout = (Tasks I need / should / have to do) > (Tasks I choose to do. If I have lot of obligations and expectation related tasks, I experience burnout even though I do a lot less. doing the other way makes me busy but surprisingly does not cause burnout.
- Really challenging tasks that I had an belief that I could not do, and then doing it small steps at a time to being able to do it has been really great and rewarding.
- Last minute panic works great - "I have 7 ways to do a thing but now I have no time, if I have to make a deadline there is no choice but to do the thing right now in a timely fashion" These are the moments I have most clarity and no hesitation in doing things. 
- Intersection of my goals and interests - I can put consistent progress here.
- Hyperfocus - works really great and I have the most progress here, but cant control when and where I hyperfocus usually.


## What had mixed results

- I also stopped being distracted (watching something) while eating/going bathroom. I built a meditation routine which I was doing everyday. Both did now work past 2-3 months and I am now completely at zero. This was really great when I was doing them, but I stopped. There are many other examples.
- Meditation was tied with my GF, we both did it, I increased my level and was more consistent, she was comparative and in the end wanted to repeat the same routine, while I wanted to move a bit advanced. She completely stopped, that reduced my frequency and I tried convincing her but over time (and maybe vacations) I completely stopped.

## What did not work

- Good habits that I do everyday, eg: for fitness, the best consistent record I had was being regular for 2 months but then a single week of not doing it and it suddenly goes to 0 progress and habit building from scratch again.
- Lists and sticky notes. Become background. I have not been able to build a system I can trust.
- Trying to sleep early. I feel bad sleeping early since I did not get enough done in the day (maybe other reasons too but this is my best guess that made sense)
- I learn/realize a lot of things but I dont remember most of them. I started writing them down too but that has not helped yet.
- Having too many options of things to do (gives the feeling of freedom), makes things worse and puts me in overthinking loop, daydreaming. I become essentially paralyzed.
- Goals that dont align with interest - hardest to do (recently actively dont do it and dont waste time overthinking. but frequently I get thoughts of I should just do it to close the loop and not have to think about it in the future)
